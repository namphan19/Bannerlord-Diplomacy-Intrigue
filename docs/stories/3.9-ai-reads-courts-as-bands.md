## 1. User story

> **As** a player whose court is the target of an AI realm's espionage,
> **I want** the AI to choose which of my lords to bribe or deceive from the same signs I could read
> of its court,
> **so that** "the AI plays by the same rules" holds for information as well as for prices and odds.

## 2. Why

The rule layer is shared: launch, odds, prices and exposure are the player's own calls. The
**choosing** is not. [AiEspionage.cs:232-263](../../src/DiplomacyIntrigue/Espionage/AiEspionage.cs#L232-L263)
reads a rival's **exact** legitimacy and each house's **exact** loyalty. A player sees a rival's
crown as Failing / Questioned / Secure and its houses in four loyalty bands; the exact figures are
what a successful `ReadCourt` sells.

Run 09 read three AI plans: **two chose a house by an exact loyalty among houses the bands show as
equal** (four "Ready to break" in Battania). None passed its gates, so no outcome changed, but the
asymmetry is real and it is the same fault R-1 fixed for the AI's war valuation.

The current thresholds already sit on band edges, with one exception:

| Constant | Value | Band edge |
|---|---|---|
| `AiSubvertLegitimacy` | 50 | `LegitimacyNeutral` 50: *Questioned* or worse |
| `AiBribeMaxLoyalty` | 40 | `LoyaltyTransactional` 40: *Disaffected* or *Defection risk* |
| `AiForgeMinLoyalty` | 25 | `LoyaltyDisaffected` 25: *Disaffected* |
| `AiForgeMaxLoyalty` | **45** | **no edge.** It reaches 5 points into *Transactional* |

So the change is mostly in **how a house is chosen within a band**, plus one constant.

## 3. Scope

**In**
- The shaky-crown test, the bribe mark and the forgery mark in `AiEspionage.PlanMission`.
- The forgery band (`AiForgeMaxLoyalty`).
- The dry run `diplomacy.ai_espionage`, which prints the reason.

**Out**
- The AI never running `ScoutArmies` and `ReadCourt`. That asymmetry predates espionage and is
  recorded in the class header; it is the subject of no decision here.
- What a *successful* `ReadCourt` would let the AI see. The AI does not run it (above).
- `SabotageGarrison`, `SpreadDissent`, `StealTreasury`, `Assassinate`: their choices read a town's
  loyalty, a treasury and an army's size, which the map or the Encyclopedia already show the player.
  Confirm this in ST-1; if any reads a figure the player sees only as a band, it is in scope.
- The peace hint that shows a rival's exact exhaustion (TODO.md, "A rival's court can be read off
  the peace hint"). That is the same band rule, but a diplomacy screen, not espionage.

## 4. Business rules

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| R1 | **A shaky crown** is one whose `CourtBands.CrownOf` is *Questioned* or *Failing*, or one with a standing pretender (pretenders are public: they are announced). This is today's test written through the band function, so it does not change who qualifies |
| R2 | **The bribe mark** is a house in the *Defection risk* band if there is one, otherwise in *Disaffected*; never *Transactional* or *Reliable*. Read through `LoyaltyModel.BandOf`, never `LoyaltyModel.Of` |
| R3 | **The forgery mark** is a house in the *Disaffected* band: close enough to the defection line that a grievance of 8 can matter, not yet over it. `AiForgeMaxLoyalty` becomes `LoyaltyTransactional` (40), and the two forge constants are replaced by a band check. The player's house is **no longer excluded** (3.10 D2): it is chosen by this same rule, and what happens on success is 3.10's offer |
| R4 | **Within a band, the tie-break uses public signs only**, in this order: (a) `CourtWeight` (a great house first: more men to take to a rising), (b) the house's relation with **our** ruler, highest first (we know our own relations exactly), (c) the clan's string id, so the choice is stable week to week and reproducible in a log |
| R5 | **The score that ranks the bribe against other operations** must not leak the exact figure either. Today it is `2 + (40 - loyalty) / 20`. It becomes a fixed score per band (*Defection risk* above *Disaffected*). The values are UN-TUNED and live in `EspionageConstants` |
| R6 | **The dry run says what it read.** `ai_espionage` prints the band and the tie-break that chose the mark, never the exact loyalty, because the reason the AI gives is the reason it acted on |
| R7 | **One resolver.** No new band function. Every read goes through `CourtBands` or `LoyaltyModel.BandOf`, the functions the Encyclopedia uses, so the AI and the page cannot drift apart |

## 5. Acceptance criteria

**AC1 — No exact figure in the choice**
- A grep of `AiEspionage.cs` finds no `LoyaltyModel.Of` and no `LegitimacyRegistry.Of` on the
  planning path (the class may still use them in log text only if that text is not the reason; prefer
  none).

**AC2 — Same marks for the same bands**
- On `di_pretender_test` (Battania, four houses "Ready to break" in run 09): `ai_espionage` names the
  mark with its band and tie-break. Moving one of those houses' exact loyalty within the band (a
  grievance via an existing lever) does **not** change the mark.

**AC3 — The forgery band**
- A house at loyalty 42 (Transactional) is never chosen for forged letters; a house at 30 is eligible.

**AC4 — Behaviour preserved where it should be**
- The set of realms the AI considers shaky is unchanged on three saves (`di_phase1_full`,
  `di_pretender_test`, `di_36_espionage_test`): before and after, printed side by side.

**AC5 — Safety**
- `build.ps1`, `compile-check.sh` pass. No save data.

## 8. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| The AI bribes a slightly less ripe house than before, and fewer bribes land | The success roll does not read loyalty, only the network, the handler and counter-intelligence, so the odds are unchanged. What changes is which house the -20 lands on, which the 3.12 run reports |
| The forgery band shrinks from 25-45 to 25-40 and AI forgeries get rarer | Report the count in 3.12. A forgery on a Transactional house was never going to reach the defection line with one grievance of 8 anyway, which is the reason the band exists |
