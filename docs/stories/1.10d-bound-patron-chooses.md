## 1. User story

> **As** the ruler of a hegemon whose vassal has been attacked by a kingdom I hold a treaty with,
> **I want** to choose between keeping my word to that kingdom and defending my vassal,
> **so that** the conflict between my two obligations is a decision I make and pay for, not a
> penalty applied to me without my having any say.

The AI patron faces the same choice under the same rules (CLAUDE.md §3).

## 2. Why

Today the case is not a choice. When vassal V is attacked by X, and patron P holds a treaty with X
that forbids war between them, `CallToArms.Applies` skips P entirely
([CallToArms.cs:195-198](../../src/DiplomacyIntrigue/Diplomacy/CallToArms.cs#L195-L198):
*"the pact it already holds with the enemy wins, and the ally is simply not called"*). V's Hold
still loses half of the protection term for that war (*legal neglect*, `HoldLegalNeglectShare` 0.5,
2026-09-27). The patron is charged for a choice it was never offered.

The lead's direction (2026-10-01): the patron chooses a side. That turns a passive penalty into a
political decision: keep faith with a neighbour, or keep a vassal.

## 3. Scope

**In**
- The case where V is the **defender** in a war against X, X is not P's own client, and P holds one
  or more active war-forbidding treaties with X (`Treaty.ForbidsWar`: non-aggression pact, truce,
  defensive pact, alliance, tributary pact).
- Both moments the patron is called today: a war declared on V (`CallToArms.Issue`) and a new
  vassal's existing defensive wars at signing (`CallToArms.DefendNewVassal`).
- The player as P (an inquiry), the AI as P (a decision function with a dry run).
- Telemetry, debug commands, and docs.

**Out**
- **Two clients of the same patron at war with each other.** Unchanged: P is not called and
  `ReconcileWithSiblings` handles it. Making P pick between its own clients is a different problem.
- **P is itself X's vassal** (the forbidding treaty is a `Vassalage` in which P is the subordinate).
  Breaking that oath is secession (design 04 §6.3), not this choice. P is not offered it, and the
  war stays *legal neglect* as today.
- Allies and defensive-pact partners that are not patrons. Their rule ("the standing treaty
  wins") is unchanged.
- The vassal's side of the call to arms (Case 1) and the summons.
- A discount on breaking a treaty to defend a vassal (D2).

## 4. Business rules

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| R1 | **When.** P is put the choice instead of being skipped, when every other `Applies` condition for a protecting patron holds and the only thing stopping the call is a war-forbidding treaty with X. If P would not be called for another reason (already at war with X, X is a sibling client, exhaustion above `CallToArmsRefuseAboveExhaustion`), nothing changes |
| R2 | **Two answers.** *Honour the vassal*: break **every** war-forbidding treaty P holds with X, then join V's war against X. *Honour the treaty*: stay out; the war counts as *legal neglect* exactly as today. There is no third answer; "stay out and keep everything" is the second one |
| R3 | **Full price for the breach** (D2). Each treaty is broken through the one existing path, `TreatyRegistry.Break`: trust lost with X and with every observing court, X gains a broken-treaty claim (a casus belli against P), and P's crown loses legitimacy when intrigue is on. No new cost and no discount. Several treaties are several breaches, each charged |
| R4 | **Order is atomic.** All the treaties are broken first, then the war is joined, in one call. If any step fails, nothing that remains is done, the failure is logged, and the war is treated as *honour the treaty*. A treaty left standing would let the Phase 1 backstops (`TreatyEnforcement.WhyWarActionRefused`) refuse the war after the trust was already paid |
| R5 | **One step only.** P joins through the call to arms, inside its `_issuing` guard, so P's own allies and vassals are not called into this war by P's joining. The same cascade rule as every other answer |
| R6 | **No stored state.** Which side P chose is read from the world, the same way `Hegemony.AnswerTo` already does: P at war with X is *Joined*, a forbidding treaty still standing is *Bound*. No flag, no save field (CLAUDE.md §1, *"a hegemon is derived, never stored"*, and §3 *one resolver per concept*) |
| R7 | **Asked once, per war.** P is asked when it is called. Choosing the treaty is not asked again for the same war. The player can still change course later through the existing break and declare actions, and the Hold term will follow on its own, because it is derived (R6) |
| R8 | **The AI decides by one function** `Hegemony.WouldHonourVassal(state, link, attacker, out why)`, and the dry run and the player's inquiry text read the same function. It weighs: <br>• **what the link is worth**: annual tribute from V against P's income, V's strength against P's, and how much Hold the link has left above the lapse line. A link already drifting toward lapse is not worth a breach <br>• **what the breach costs**: the trust P would lose with X and with observers, the claim X would gain, P's legitimacy, and what the treaty with X is worth (an alliance more than a truce; tribute P *receives* from X counts in full) <br>• **the odds**: the same whole-side comparison and `CallToArmsHopelessRatio` the call to arms already uses. A war P cannot win is refused whatever the link is worth <br>The weights are constants in `DiplomacyConstants.cs`, **UN-TUNED** until ST-6 |
| R9 | **Player as P.** An inquiry names both answers and their full price: each treaty that would be broken with its trust and claim cost, and the Hold the vassal would lose from neglect. The AI's choice for the same situation is not shown to the player |
| R10 | **Player as X or V.** No new prompt. V is told which side its patron chose. If P breaks with the player's kingdom X, the existing broken-treaty notification fires |
| R11 | **The comment that becomes wrong is fixed.** `CallToArms.cs:195-197` states the opposite rule for patrons. It is rewritten to state the new one and why (CLAUDE.md §5) |

## 5. Acceptance criteria

**AC1 — The choice is offered only in its case**
- Given V attacked by X, P holding a truce with X, no other bar: P is asked (player) or decides
  (AI), and the log writes `[PROTECT] bound-choice patron=P vassal=V attacker=X treaties=Truce`.
- Given X is another client of P, or P is X's vassal, or P is already at war with X: no choice
  is offered and behaviour is exactly as before this story.
- An ally or defensive-pact partner bound to X is still simply not called.

**AC2 — Honour the vassal**
- Every forbidding treaty between P and X is closed `Broken` with P as breaker, each charged once
  through `TreatyRegistry.Break`, and P is at war with X the same day.
- `diplomacy.hegemony` shows the war under *protection* (joined), not *legal neglect*.
- P's own allies and vassals are not called into this war by P's joining.

**AC3 — Honour the treaty**
- Nothing is broken, P stays out, and `diplomacy.hegemony` shows the war under *legal neglect*
  as today, with the same number.

**AC4 — Atomic**
- With a forced failure in the war step (a test lever), no treaty is left half-broken without
  the war, or the log says exactly which treaties were broken before the failure and the war
  is counted as *honour the treaty*.

**AC5 — AI decision is explainable**
- `diplomacy.bound_choice <patron> | <vassal> | <attacker>` prints every term of R8 and the
  answer, and that answer is the one the AI takes in the same state.

**AC6 — Player inquiry**
- The inquiry lists each treaty that would be broken and its cost, and the Hold cost of staying
  out. Each answer does exactly what AC2 or AC3 describes. Use `affirmative`, not `accept`, when
  driving it through the bridge (CLAUDE.md §2).

**AC7 — Balance**
- In a 10-year run: both answers occur at least once; the share of `DefendAlly` plus obligation
  wars stays under ~35 % (design 04 §10); no hegemon loses all its treaties to this rule alone
  (report the count of treaties broken by this rule per hegemon).

**AC8 — Safety**
- No exception crosses the engine boundary. On an exception the patron is treated as *honour the
  treaty* and the game carries on.
- `scripts/check-save-ids.ps1` passes with no change (this story adds no save data).

## 7. Decisions

| # | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Should the patron choose a side when bound to its vassal's attacker? | **Decided 2026-10-01 (lead): yes.** This story |
| D2 | Is breaking a treaty to defend a vassal cheaper than other breaches? | **No discount** (tech lead's proposal, confirmed with the direction). A cheaper breach would make vassalage a way to tear up any treaty cheaply; the full price is what makes the choice weigh |
| D3 | Two clients of the same patron at war: does the patron choose between them? | **No.** Unchanged, `ReconcileWithSiblings` |
| D4 | Should the AI ever change its mind later in the same war (re-evaluate weekly)? | Default **no** (R7). Open if ST-6 shows patrons stranded in wars that turned |

## 8. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| More wars, and alliances torn up in chains, as patrons break treaties to defend vassals | Full breach price (D2), the hopeless-odds bar, one-step cascade (R5), AC7's war-share check |
| Run 09's numbers stop describing the game | Ship after run 09's analysis is written; say in the run report which build had this rule |
| A treaty left standing blocks the war after trust was paid | Atomic order (R4), AC4 |
| The player is asked in an unattended run and the clock stops | As today: balance runs keep the player out of a patron's seat |
| `WouldHonourVassal` weights picked by hand | UN-TUNED marks, dry-run command, ST-6 measures both answers occur |
