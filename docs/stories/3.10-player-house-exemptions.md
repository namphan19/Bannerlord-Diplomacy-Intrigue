## 1. User story

> **As** a player whose realm is a rival's target,
> **I want** the AI's espionage to reach my house under the same rules it uses on every other house,
> **so that** my house is not the one safe house in Calradia, and what I can do to others can be done
> to me.

## 2. Why

Two exemptions are in the code today:

| # | Exemption | Where |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | The AI never **assassinates** anyone of the player's house | `FieldCommander`, `AiEspionage.cs:510` |
| E2 | The AI never sends **forged letters** to the player's house | `PlanMission`, `AiEspionage.cs:250` |

Neither is a rule the player can see or play against. The lead's decision (2026-10-01):
**E1 is removed outright. E2 becomes an offer**, the same shape decision 12 already gave an AI bribe
that reaches the player.

## 3. Scope

**In**
- E1: removed. The player's house, the player hero included, is a mark under the AI's ordinary rule.
- E2: a successful forgery on the player's house opens an inquiry; the player chooses.
- The class header of `AiEspionage`, design 03 §9 and §10, TODO.md.

**Out**
- The bribe offer to the player (decision 12). It exists and works (3.6 check 4); E2 copies its shape
  and does not change it.
- `AiEspionage.cs:102` (no planning for a player-ruled realm) and `:373` (the player's orphaned
  network not wound down). Both concern the player's *own* espionage, not the AI's targets.
- Any change to the AI's assassination rule itself: at war only, the commander of the target's
  largest army in the field, never its ruler (decision 11). That stays exactly as it is.

## 4. Business rules

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| R1 | **E1 removed.** Line 510 (`if (leader.Clan == Clan.PlayerClan) continue;`) is deleted. `FieldCommander` treats the player's house as any other: the largest army's commander, never the ruler. **The player hero is a mark** when they command that army and do not rule the realm. The doc comment on `FieldCommander` and the class header say so, citing this story |
| R2 | **The player hero's death is vanilla's.** A successful assassination of `Hero.MainHero` goes through the same `KillCharacterAction.ApplyByMurder` as any victim. Vanilla's own main-hero death path (heir selection, or the end of the campaign with no heir) follows. The mod adds no protection and no special case |
| R3 | **The player is told.** When an assassination succeeds on anyone of the player's house, the player is notified in red, without naming who ordered it ("<name> is dead. Nobody knows who ordered it" is the owner's text; the victim's side needs its own line). An exposed attempt is already reported by `Exposure.Apply` |
| R4 | **E2 as an offer.** `PlanMission` no longer skips the player's house for forgery (line 250's `clan != Clan.PlayerClan` is removed; the band rule of 3.9 applies). A successful roll on the player's house does **not** apply the grievance; it marks the mission `OfferOwed` and opens an inquiry, exactly as `Resolve` does for a bribe on `Hero.MainHero` |
| R5 | **The inquiry.** Title and body in the bribe offer's style. The body says what the letters claim: that the player's ruler means to wrong the house (the grievance the letters would create, in words), and what believing them does: "a grievance of 8 against <ruler>, which lowers our loyalty to the crown". It does **not** say the letters are forged or who sent them. Two answers: *Believe them* and *Dismiss them* |
| R6 | ***Believe them***: the grievance is recorded as for any house (`GrievanceType.ForgedLetters`, weight 8). On the player's own Court tab it shows as the grievance the player chose to believe; whether the ledger label reads "forged" there is ST-1's call, stated in this story before coding (the player would learn the truth from the label) |
| R7 | ***Dismiss them***: no grievance. The mission is recorded as a **Failure** with no exposure roll: the letters reached a reader who did not believe them, nobody was caught. The network pays a failure's cost, as for any failure |
| R8 | **Reload and queueing reuse the bribe's machinery.** `OfferOwed` (property 12) covers a save made while the offer is open; the `_askingPlayer` guard covers a second offer arriving while one is up. Generalise the guard and the "ask again" path so both mission types share them; do not copy them |
| R9 | **No timeout answers for the player.** If the inquiry is given a timeout, the timeout answers *Dismiss them*: silence cannot make the player believe something (the same reasoning 1.10d used for its timeout) |
| R10 | **The AI chooses as before.** Nothing in the AI's choice reads "is this the player": the player's house is chosen or not by the same band and tie-break as every house (3.9) |

## 5. Acceptance criteria

**AC1 — The code says what was decided**
- A grep for `Clan.PlayerClan` and `Hero.MainHero` in `AiEspionage.cs` returns only line 102's and
  line 373's checks (Out), each with a comment.

**AC2 — Assassination reaches the player's house**
- Staged: the player is a vassal commanding the largest army of a realm at war with an AI realm whose
  ruling house has a network on it (`test_set_network`). `ai_espionage` names the player hero as the
  mark. With a companion of the player's house commanding instead, it names the companion.
- `test_launch_mission` + `test_resolve_mission ... | success` on a **companion**: dead, the player told (R3).
- On the **player hero**, on a throwaway save with an heir: vanilla's heir path runs, no exception in
  the mod log or `rgl_log_errors_*`. **And** on one with no heir: the campaign ends on vanilla's
  screen, no crash. Record both; never on a test save anyone needs afterwards.

**AC3 — Forgery as an offer**
- Staged on a shaky realm with the player as a vassal in the Disaffected band:
  `test_launch_mission <AI ruling house> | <realm> | ForgeLetters | <player>`, then
  `test_resolve_mission ... | success`. The inquiry opens; read with `ui/get_inquiry`.
- *Believe*: `diplomacy.grievances` shows `ForgedLetters 8.0` on the player's house; the Court tab row.
- *Dismiss*: no grievance; `diplomacy.missions` shows the mission as Failure; no exposure.
- Answer through the bridge with `affirmative` true/false, never `accept` (CLAUDE.md §2).
- Save while the inquiry is open, reload in a fresh process: the offer is asked again, not rolled again.

**AC4 — Safety**
- No exception crosses the engine boundary. `build.ps1`, `compile-check.sh` pass;
  `check-save-ids.ps1` unchanged.

**AC5 — Documents**
- design 03 §9 records both decisions with the date; decision 11's text says the player's house is no
  longer exempt; TODO.md items 3 and 4 are closed with a pointer here.

## 8. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| A campaign ends on one unseen roll | The lead's decision, accepted knowingly. The odds are the player's own (`Missions.OddsOf`), the AI only assassinates at war and needs 70 network strength and 60,000 denars, and counter-intelligence is the player's defence. The 3.12 run reports how often the player hero was a mark |
| Vanilla's main-hero death path breaks on a death by murder outside a battle | AC2 runs both the heir and the no-heir case on throwaway saves |
| The forgery inquiry stops the clock in an unattended run | As for the bribe: balance runs keep the player out of reach (3.12 §8) |
