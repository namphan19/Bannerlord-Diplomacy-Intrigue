## 1. User story

> **As** a player whose realm is a rival's target,
> **I want** the AI's espionage to reach my house under the same rules it uses on every other house,
> **so that** my house is not the one safe house in Calradia, and what I can do to others can be done
> to me.

## 2. Why

Two exemptions are in the code today:

| # | Exemption | Where |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | The AI never **assassinates** anyone of the player's house | `FieldCommander`, `AiEspionage.cs:510` |
| E2 | The AI never sends **forged letters** to the player's house | `PlanMission`, `AiEspionage.cs:250` |

Neither is a rule the player can see or play against. The lead's decision (2026-10-01):
**E1 is removed outright. E2 becomes an offer**, the same shape decision 12 already gave an AI bribe
that reaches the player.

## 3. Scope

**In**
- E1: removed. The player's house, the player hero included, is a mark under the AI's ordinary rule.
- E2: a successful forgery on the player's house opens an inquiry; the player chooses.
- The class header of `AiEspionage`, design 03 §9 and §10, TODO.md.

**Out**
- The bribe offer to the player (decision 12). It exists and works (3.6 check 4); E2 copies its shape
  and does not change it.
- `AiEspionage.cs:102` (no planning for a player-ruled realm) and `:373` (the player's orphaned
  network not wound down). Both concern the player's *own* espionage, not the AI's targets.
- Any change to the AI's assassination rule itself: at war only, the commander of the target's
  largest army in the field, never its ruler (decision 11). That stays exactly as it is.

## 4. Business rules

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| R1 | **E1 removed.** Line 510 (`if (leader.Clan == Clan.PlayerClan) continue;`) is deleted. `FieldCommander` treats the player's house as any other: the largest army's commander, never the ruler. **The player hero is a mark** when they command that army and do not rule the realm. The doc comment on `FieldCommander` and the class header say so, citing this story |
| R2 | **The player hero's death is vanilla's.** A successful assassination of `Hero.MainHero` goes through the same `KillCharacterAction.ApplyByMurder` as any victim. Vanilla's own main-hero death path (heir selection, or the end of the campaign with no heir) follows. The mod adds no protection and no special case |
| R3 | **The player is told.** When an assassination succeeds on anyone of the player's house, the player is notified in red, without naming who ordered it ("<name> is dead. Nobody knows who ordered it" is the owner's text; the victim's side needs its own line). An exposed attempt is already reported by `Exposure.Apply` |
| R4 | **E2 as an offer.** `PlanMission` no longer skips the player's house for forgery (line 250's `clan != Clan.PlayerClan` is removed; the band rule of 3.9 applies). A successful roll on the player's house does **not** apply the grievance; it marks the mission `OfferOwed` and opens an inquiry, exactly as `Resolve` does for a bribe on `Hero.MainHero` |
| R5 | **The inquiry.** Title and body in the bribe offer's style. The body says what the letters claim: that the player's ruler means to wrong the house (the grievance the letters would create, in words), and what believing them does: "a grievance of 8 against <ruler>, which lowers our loyalty to the crown". It does **not** say the letters are forged or who sent them. Two answers: *Believe them* and *Dismiss them* |
| R6 | ***Believe them***: the grievance is recorded as for any house (`GrievanceType.ForgedLetters`, weight 8). On the player's own Court tab it shows as the grievance the player chose to believe; whether the ledger label reads "forged" there is ST-1's call, stated in this story before coding (the player would learn the truth from the label) |
| R7 | ***Dismiss them***: no grievance. The mission is recorded as a **Failure** with no exposure roll: the letters reached a reader who did not believe them, nobody was caught. The network pays a failure's cost, as for any failure |
| R8 | **Reload and queueing reuse the bribe's machinery.** `OfferOwed` (property 12) covers a save made while the offer is open; the `_askingPlayer` guard covers a second offer arriving while one is up. Generalise the guard and the "ask again" path so both mission types share them; do not copy them |
| R9 | **No timeout answers for the player.** If the inquiry is given a timeout, the timeout answers *Dismiss them*: silence cannot make the player believe something (the same reasoning 1.10d used for its timeout) |
| R10 | **The AI chooses as before.** Nothing in the AI's choice reads "is this the player": the player's house is chosen or not by the same band and tie-break as every house (3.9) |

  the mod log or `rgl_log_errors_*`. **And** on one with no heir: the campaign ends on vanilla's
  screen, no crash. Record both; never on a test save anyone needs afterwards.

**AC3 — Forgery as an offer**
- Staged on a shaky realm with the player as a vassal in the Disaffected band:
  `test_launch_mission <AI ruling house> | <realm> | ForgeLetters | <player>`, then

- *Dismiss*: no grievance; `diplomacy.missions` shows the mission as Failure; no exposure.
- Answer through the bridge with `affirmative` true/false, never `accept` (CLAUDE.md §2).
- Save while the inquiry is open, reload in a fresh process: the offer is asked again, not rolled again.

## 9. ST-1 — the two questions this story had to answer before coding

Both read against the **local v1.5.3 install by IL**, on 2026-10-01. What v1.4.8 does was not
checked and is not claimed.

### R6's ledger label — decided: the label changes, and says what the court was told

`GrievanceRegistry.TitleOf` reads `"Letters in the crown's hand - forged"`, and its comment gives
the reason: *"Named as forged. The Court tab is the victim's own court, which was told so when the
letters surfaced."* For every court until now that sentence was true — the letters arrived under
the crown's seal from a foreign agent, the victim was told they were forged, and naming them so is
honest. With 3.10 the player is told **nothing**: R5 says the inquiry does not say the letters are
forged, and R7 leaves the mission a plain Failure when they are dismissed. A ledger that then
reads "forged" hands the player the answer the story just made them pay to guess, and the row
becomes a lie about what that court knows.

**Decision: the label for the player's own court reads `"Letters in the crown's hand"` — the
words on the paper, not a verdict on them.** It is the label the player's court can actually
justify, and it matches what the inquiry told them. The mod does not tell them the letters were
forged unless the operation is **exposed**, which is the rule design 03 §5 already sets for
everything else an operation does to the player: a harm you can see, a culprit you learn only when
the network is caught.

One resolver, so this cannot become two truths: the wording is decided **by whether the holder is
the player's clan**, inside `TitleOf`, which is the single place every reader goes through - the
Court tab, the Encyclopedia ledger under a `ReadCourt`, and the amends messages. Not a second
string at the call site, and not a new `GrievanceType` (a new value on an existing enum is safe;
a new type would be a save-id question for no gain).

What the player **does** still learn is that someone is lying to them: the label now reads as any
other slight from a court they do not trust, and the exposure path names the forger. That is the
same information a real court would have.

### R3's notification — decided: vanilla does not notify, so the mod does

Checked, because ST-1 asks whether R3 is already satisfied by vanilla's death notice. It is not.

`KillCharacterAction.ApplyInternal` branches at IL_00e6: if the victim is `IsHumanPlayerCharacter`
**and** the action is not forced, it fires `OnBeforeMainCharacterDied` and **returns** - it never
reaches `MakeDead`, `ChangeClanLeaderAction`, or `Hero.OnDeath` on that path. Two receivers act on
it, both in `SandBox.dll`:

- `HeirSelectionCampaignBehavior.OnBeforeMainCharacterDied` - collects `MainHero.Clan.GetHeirApparents()`.
  **Empty** (no heir): `OnGameOver`, `GameOverCleanup`, `ShowGameStatistics`, `Campaign.OnGameOver` -
  the campaign ends, on vanilla's own Game Over screen. **Not empty**: `EndCaptivityAction.ApplyByDeath`
  if the hero was a prisoner, then `OnHeirSelectionRequested` - the vanilla heir prompt.
- `DefaultCutscenesCampaignBehavior.OnBeforeMainCharacterDied` - builds a scene notification, and
  only for four cases: at sea (`NavalDeathSceneNotificationItem`), `DiedOfOldAge` (3),
  `DiedInBattle` (4), `Executed` (6). **`Murdered` (1) is not among them**, so an assassination of
  the player hero reaches `ldloc.0` as null and shows nothing at all.

**Two facts follow, and both are load-bearing:**

1. **R2 holds, and it is vanilla's path end to end.** `ApplyByMurder` -> `ApplyInternal` ->
   `OnBeforeMainCharacterDied` -> either the heir prompt or Game Over. The mod adds nothing and
   must add nothing. AC2's two throwaway-save runs are still owed, because IL is not a run.
2. **R3 needs the mod's own line.** Nothing in vanilla announces a murder. The mod's `TellVictim`
   already covers the other three operations; assassinate never called it. It does now, in red,
   naming the victim and not the payer - the same rule `TellOwner` follows in the other direction.

The text R3 specifies for the owner, *"X is dead. Nobody knows who ordered it"*, is the owner's.
The victim's side needs its own, and it is the mirror image with the blame removed: who died, and
that nobody has been caught.

### Also checked, because AC2 depends on it

`ApplyInternal` IL_00e9 returns early when `victim.IsHumanPlayerCharacter` **and** `isForced` is
true. `ApplyByMurder` passes `isForced: false` (IL_0004, `ldc.i4.0`), so the mod's call does reach
`OnBeforeMainCharacterDied` and the heir path - it is not silently short-circuited. That is the
difference between the game ending and a hero quietly not dying, and it is worth one line of
comment where the mod calls it.

### AC1 - the code says what was decided: **PASS**

A grep of `AiEspionage.cs` for `Clan.PlayerClan` and `Hero.MainHero` returns **two** lines, both

### AC2, AC3 - **not verified. The live checks are blocked, and the blocker is story 3.8's.**

Both need an AI ruling house with a network on the realm holding the player. Staging that by hand
was attempted on three saves and **failed the same way every time**:

| Save | What was staged | Result |
|---|---|---|
| `di_pretender_test` | player as a vassal house of Aserai | `test_player_join` refused: the player's house rules Khuzait |
| `run10_player_patron` | - | the player **rules Vlandia** there, so it is a ruling clan and can never be a mark |
| `di_civilwar_2_6c` | `test_concede Battania \| crown` put the player on the other side, and `diplomacy.loyalty Battania` then showed **Airit (Battania) at loyalty 23.2, a defection risk** - exactly AC3's staging | `test_assign_handler` refused for **every** ruling house: Decantia, Jinda and Dhiyul already run a network, Asta and Andruta govern towns, and the rest lead parties. No network on Battania could be founded, so `test_launch_mission ... \| Airit` answered *"Osticos has no network in Battania"* |

**The player-side staging worked; the AI-side did not, and never can today.** `test_assign_handler`
goes through the same `SpyNetworks.Assign` the weekly run uses, so it hits the same wall run 09 §4
found: an AI house has at most one handler and every free lord of a ruling clan is already
governing, leading a party or posted abroad. This is **story 3.8's blocker**, not a fault in 3.10 -
and it means 3.10 cannot be verified at all until 3.8 lands.

**What that costs, stated plainly:**

- **AC2 (assassination reaches the player's house)** - unrun. The exemption is gone from the code
  and the IL says vanilla's death path is sound, but "the AI names the player as the mark" has not
  been seen, and neither the heir case nor the no-heir case has been run. Both must run on
  throwaway saves, and the no-heir case ends the campaign by design.
- **AC3 (forgery as an offer)** - unrun. The player-side state was reached and read
  (`Airit at 23.2`, a defection risk, the mark the band rule would choose), and the inquiry's two
  answers, the grievance, the Failure record and the reload were **not** exercised.
- The label change in §9 was **not** seen on the Court tab either, for the same reason: it needs
  the player to believe letters, which needs the offer.

### What would unblock it

`diplomacy.test_set_network` sets a network's strength but cannot found one. A founding lever that
does not go through `Assign`'s eligibility rules would stage AC2/AC3 in two commands. **That is a
change to a test lever, not to the game's rules** - the alternative is waiting on 3.8 and re-testing
there, which is the plan if the lead would rather not add a lever that bypasses a rule the AI obeys.
Raised rather than decided, because a lever that skips `CanHandle` is the kind of thing that makes a
later check lie.

**Decided 2026-10-02 (the lead, on the tech lead's recommendation): the lever is added.**
`diplomacy.test_found_network <hero> | <kingdom> [| strength]`, cheat-gated, through
`SpyNetworks.AssignForTest`. It skips only the "free to go abroad" rules (clan head, party, governor,
travelling, fugitive, released) and moves nobody; it keeps every rule whose breach would make a later
check lie about something else (alive, grown, free, of the clan, a foreign realm with a town, no second
network). The arrangement lasts until the next daily tick, which releases a busy hero or fetches a free
one - so AC2 and AC3 are staged as `test_found_network` -> `test_launch_mission` ->
`test_resolve_mission ... | success`, with no `tick_days` in between. Built and compiled against v1.5.3
and the v1.4.8 references; not run.
