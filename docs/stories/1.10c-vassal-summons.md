## 1. User story

> **As** the ruler of a kingdom that holds other kingdoms as vassals (a hegemon),
> **I want** to order some of my vassals' war parties to march under my command,
> **so that** I can see my vassals serving me on the map, and their loyalty (Hold) decides whether
> they come or not.

A second story follows from the rule that the AI plays by the same rules (CLAUDE.md §3):

> **As** a player whose kingdom is a vassal,
> **I want** my patron's summons to reach me as a choice with a price on each answer,
> **so that** serving or defying my patron is a decision I make.

## 2. Why now

- Today a hegemon gets tribute, control of the vassal's foreign policy, and a call to arms. The
  call to arms makes the vassal fight **alongside** the hegemon, in parallel. Nothing puts a
  vassal's lords **under** the hegemon on the map.
- Hold already has a "refuses summons" tier (30–39, design 04 §6.1). The code already turns a
  refusal into a defiance mark (`CallToArms.cs`), and `PLAYER-GUIDE.md` §Hold, `DiplomacyMenu`
  and `release/nexus-description.bbcode` all name it. This story builds the summons those texts
  describe.
- The 2026-09-24 review parked it as "least load-bearing"
  ([02-unbuilt-mechanics.md](../reviews/2026-09-24-mechanics/02-unbuilt-mechanics.md)). The lead
  re-opened it on 2026-09-30.

## 3. Scope

**In**
- The patron's **ruler** issues a summons to one vassal kingdom **that is serving it in an
  obligation war** (it answered the call to arms). The summons takes up to half of that vassal's
  war parties under the patron's command, for a fixed time or until that war ends. A summons is
  the call to arms carried one step further: from fighting *beside* the patron to fighting *under*
  it (lead, 2026-10-01).
- The price follows the lead's rule of 2026-09-26: influence **and** gold, each part scaled by skill.
- The answer depends on Hold: serve, or refuse and earn a defiance mark.
- AI hegemons use it under the same rules and caps.
- UI: the player gives the order from the Realm tab and answers a summons through an inquiry.
- Telemetry, debug commands, and updates to the player guide and handbook.

**Out**
- Summoning another kingdom's **ruler's own party**. The vassal's ruler always stays free.
- Summoning parties of a kingdom that is **not** a vassal (an ally, for example). Allies keep the call to arms.
- A summons in peacetime, or in any war other than the vassal's current obligation war.
- **Reverse summons**, a vassal calling its patron's parties. The patron's duty to protect is
  already carried by the call to arms and the Hold protection term; the lead judged that enough
  (2026-10-01).
- Titles (Emperor, Khagan). These are separate and wait for Phase 2.4.
- Retrofitting older acts to the new pricing rule.

## 4. Business rules

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| R1 | Only the ruler of a kingdom that is the dominant party of an active `Vassalage` treaty may summon, and only that treaty's vassal. Hegemony stays derived (`Hegemony.IsHegemon`); **no stored flag** |
| R2 | A summons takes at most **⌈N/2⌉** of the vassal's eligible war parties, nearest to the summoner first (the same rule as the §5.4 cascade cap). A party is not eligible if it is led by the vassal's ruler, is in an army already, is besieged, a prisoner, or disbanding |
| R3 | **Inside an obligation war only, and enemies in common only.** A summons names the war: the one obligation war the vassal is serving its patron in (`CallToArms`, at most one at a time per vassal). A vassal that refused that call, was excused from it, or was cut by the §5.4 cap cannot be summoned for it. The patron may also be at war with a kingdom the vassal is at peace with; a summoned party never fights that kingdom, and is released from the army before it would (the mechanism is ST-3's, checked live by ST-7). Bannerlord resolves hostility by each party's own map faction, so a party whose kingdom is at peace with the target would either stand aside or break the peace |
| R4 | **The answer depends on Hold, asked again at issue** through the same `Hegemony.WouldServe` the call to arms uses (one resolver). Hold may have fallen since the vassal answered the call. Hold ≥ 40 (`HoldPassiveResistanceThreshold`): the vassal serves. Hold < 40: the vassal refuses, and the existing defiance-mark path runs (the patron's trust in the vassal falls by 15, `TrustCallToArmsRefused`; one defiance mark; a refusal that brings the marks to two renounces the vassalage at once, through `TreatyRegistry.Break`, which charges the vassal a full breach on top (−35 with the patron, −12 with every court; −50.1 measured live), and two marks stop it renewing at its term. Marks are forgotten `DefianceMarkMemoryDays` (84, one campaign year) after the last. Corrected 2026-10-01: this row said −10 trust and "lapses at its next expiry", neither of which the code does). The AI vassal decides by this rule only. A **player** vassal always chooses (R9) |
| R5 | **Excused** without a mark, under the same reasons as the call to arms: exhaustion > `VassalExcusedAboveExhaustion`, an internal war in the vassal's realm, the vassal's own territory under siege, or (D7) the vassal defending a war its patron stays out of, ignored or bound. The price is **not** charged when the vassal is excused |
| R6 | **Price** = influence + gold, both high. Influence is scaled by `StatecraftTerms.PriceFactor(patronRuler, vassalRuler, Leadership)`, the contest that design 08 already assigns to "how firmly its vassals hold". Gold is scaled by `PriceFactor(patron treasurer, Trade)`. It is charged at issue. A refusal refunds nothing. That the patron pays for its own lost face is intentional, and a question for the lead (D3) |
| R7 | **Duration and cooldown.** A summons lasts `SummonsDurationDays` or until its obligation war ends, whichever comes first, then the parties are released back to their own AI. The next summons to the same vassal is possible after `SummonsCooldownDays`. Only one summons per vassal can be live at a time |
| R8 | **It ends early** if the link ends (lapse, break, secession, poaching, dissolution), if either kingdom stops existing, if the vassal makes peace or war that R3 no longer covers, or if the summoner stops being ruler. Released parties go back to their own AI the same day |
| R9 | **Player as vassal**: the summons opens an inquiry naming the price of each answer ("Send N parties" / "Refuse: −15 trust, a defiance mark"). Parties of the **player's own clan** are never taken without that consent. An unanswered inquiry stops the clock (CLAUDE.md §2), which is acceptable because the inquiry is the player's decision |
| R10 | **AI as hegemon**: it summons only when it is marshalling an army in an obligation war the vassal is serving in (R3), when the vassal has Hold ≥ 40, and when the price is under a share of its treasury and influence (`SummonsAiBudgetShare`). It sends at most one summons per week per hegemon. The number shown to the player is the number the AI used |
| R11 | Every constant lives in `DiplomacyConstants.cs` and is marked **UN-TUNED** in its doc comment until a balance run has measured it |

**Starting values (UN-TUNED, the lead signs off on them in D2)**

| Constant | Value | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| `SummonsInfluenceBase` | 150 | Median AI ruler holds 2,374 influence (run 08). One summons should cost a real share of that |
| `SummonsInfluencePerParty` | 40 | Of the order of vanilla's per-party army call cost, but on a foreign lord |
| `SummonsGoldBase` | 20,000 | Median AI ruler holds 551k gold. Gold alone never bites, so it is paired with influence |
| `SummonsGoldPerParty` | 5,000 | Upkeep of a foreign lord's men |
| `SummonsDurationDays` | 20 | About one campaign |
| `SummonsCooldownDays` | 42 | Six weeks, the same scale as other diplomatic cooldowns |
| `SummonsAiBudgetShare` | 0.15 | No AI spends more than this share of either currency on one summons |

## 5. Acceptance criteria

Given / When / Then. "Log" means the mod log in `Documents\...\DiplomacyIntrigue\Logs\`.

**AC1 — Who may summon**
- Given the player rules a kingdom with one active vassalage, when the player opens the Realm tab,
  then each vassal row shows a **Summon** button with its price, the number of parties it would
  take, and the reason it is disabled when it is.
- Given the player is a lord but not the ruler, or the kingdom has no vassal, then no Summon
  button appears.

**AC2 — Price**
- The price the button shows is exactly what is deducted, in both currencies.
- `diplomacy.summons_value <patron> | <vassal>` prints the same numbers term by term (base,
  per party, both skill factors).
- With the statecraft switch off (`test_statecraft off`), both factors are 1.0.

**AC3 — The vassal serves (Hold ≥ 40)**
- Up to ⌈N/2⌉ eligible parties move to the summoner, nearest first. They stay under its
  command for `SummonsDurationDays`, and then return to their own AI.
- The vassal ruler's own party is never taken.
- The log writes `[SUMMONS] issued`, `[SUMMONS] served n=<k>`, and later `[SUMMONS] released reason=expired`.

**AC4 — The vassal refuses (Hold < 40)**
- No party moves. The vassal receives exactly one defiance mark and −15 trust (`TrustCallToArmsRefused`), through the
  existing path, not a copy of it. A second mark inside the memory window triggers the lapse
  that is already built.
- The log writes `[SUMMONS] refused hold=<h>`.

**AC5 — Excused**
- A vassal that meets R5 is excused: no mark, no charge. The log names the reason.

**AC6 — Enemies in common (R3)**
- A summoned party never attacks, and never joins a battle against, a faction its own kingdom is
  at peace with. No summons creates or breaks a war or a treaty.

**AC7 — Early end (R8)**
- Breaking the vassalage while a summons is live (`break_treaty`) releases every summoned party
  on the next daily tick, and the log gives the reason.

**AC8 — Save and load**
- Save during a live summons, reload: the summons is still live with the same end date, and the
  parties still follow the summoner, or are re-attached on load.
- Load a save made **before** this story: no crash, no summons, cooldowns empty.
- `scripts/check-save-ids.ps1` passes. Every new id is taken from the free list in CLAUDE.md §3.

**AC9 — Player as vassal**
- A player vassal receives an inquiry with both answers priced. "Refuse" gives the same result
  as AC4. "Send" gives the same result as AC3, and only with this consent are parties of the
  player's own clan taken.

**AC10 — AI uses it, within limits**
- In a 10-year balance run, AI hegemons issue summons (count > 0). No hegemon has more than one
  summons live per vassal, and none spends more than `SummonsAiBudgetShare` per summons.
- The share of wars that are `DefendAlly` or obligation wars stays under ~35 % (the design 04 §10 check).

**AC11 — No throw crosses the engine boundary**
- Every new hook catches, logs and continues. On an exception the summons is released and the
  game carries on as vanilla.

**AC12 — War Sails**
- The Realm tab button renders and works with NavalDLC loaded (save `di_naval_test`).

## 7. Open decisions for the lead

| # | Question | Default if not answered |
|---|---|---|
| **D1** | Must summoned parties join a **real Army** (army screen, merged on the map, sieges together)? Or is **escorting and fighting alongside** the ruler enough? | **Decided 2026-09-30 (tech lead, default applied): a real Army.** ST-1 proved it safe — design 04 §5.2a. Escort kept as the documented fallback |
| D2 | The starting prices and durations in §4 | Build with them, tune after ST-8 |
| D3 | A refused summons: does the patron get its price back? | No refund |
| D4 | May a **player** hegemon summon from a vassal whose Hold is under 40, knowing it will be refused, to force a mark on purpose? | Yes. It is a legitimate lever and it costs the full price |
| D5 | Is a summons a separate act, or the call to arms carried further? | **Decided 2026-10-01 (lead): the call to arms carried further.** Only inside the vassal's current obligation war (R3) |
| D6 | May a vassal summon its patron's parties in return? | **Decided 2026-10-01 (lead): no.** The protection duty that exists is enough |
| D7 | A vassal that is under attack while its patron stays out of that war (ignored, or bound by a treaty with the attacker - *legal neglect*): may the patron still summon its parties? | **Direction from the lead 2026-10-01: the patron chooses a side** - honour the vassal (break its treaty with the attacker and join) or honour the treaty (stay out, legal neglect as today). Built as its own story, [1.10d](1.10d-bound-patron-chooses.md), since it changes the call to arms rather than the summons. **For this story (confirmed by the lead 2026-10-01):** a vassal its patron left alone is **excused** from a summons, no mark, no charge (uses `Hegemony.AnswerTo`'s Ignored/Bound, one resolver). This story does not wait for 1.10d |
| D8 | How the summons cap sits with the §5.4 cascade cap, whether a summons weighs on the vassal's Hold, and whether one patron army has a ceiling on summoned parties | **Decided 2026-10-01 (lead).** The caps nest: §5.4 picks the vassals in the war, the summons takes ⌈n/2⌉ of one serving vassal's parties, and a vassal serves one obligation war at a time. **No new Hold term** for a live summons in the first version; ST-8 measures whether one is needed. **No ceiling** on summoned parties per army: vanilla's per-party influence cost and cohesion upkeep, on top of our price, are the limit |

## 8. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Vanilla assumes every army member shares a kingdom (the same class of bug as the ~25 unchecked `MapFaction` → `Kingdom` casts, CLAUDE.md §1) | **Answered 2026-09-30: it does not, on this path.** ST-1, design 04 §5.2a. The live half is still owed to ST-7 |
| Escort state is not saved by vanilla, so parties wander off after a reload | **The premise is wrong** — `army`, `defaultBehavior`, `moveTargetParty`, `targetPosition` and `DoNotMakeNewDecisions` are all saveable. ST-2 still stores the record, but for the end date and the parties, not to repair vanilla |
| AI hegemons strip their vassals and snowball | The ⌈N/2⌉ cap, the cooldown, the budget share, and the refusal below Hold 40. ST-8 measures it |
| A summoned party pulled into a battle with a neutral faction | R3 guard, checked by AC6 |
| An inquiry to a player vassal stalls an unattended run | Balance runs keep the player out of vassalages, as today |
